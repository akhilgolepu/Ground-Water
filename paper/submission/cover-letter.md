Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “Monsoon forcing, depth-aware calibration and decision value
in national-scale groundwater forecasting for India”, for publication in *Hydrology and Earth System
Sciences*.

Machine-learning forecasts of groundwater levels are increasingly proposed for water management in
India, but their usefulness depends on three things that have not been tested at national scale:
whether monsoon rainfall adds skill beyond the memory of the aquifer, whether forecast uncertainty can
be trusted for shallow and deep wells alike, and whether the warnings are worth acting on. Using about
414 000 readings from 6 438 Central Ground Water Board wells, gridded IMD forcing and a time-blocked
design, we find that:

1. Observed monsoon forcing adds a small but robust gain to groundwater memory, largest at a 6-month
   lead, and a perfect rainfall forecast would add 5.5–9.6 % more. The rainfall field distributed with
   the widely used public compilation of these data is a single-day snapshot, which explains a recent
   null result.
2. A single conformal interval covers 88 % of readings in shallow wells but under half in deep wells;
   intervals pooled by depth and region and updated online with adaptive conformal inference keep every
   class within about 3 points of nominal through a shift from dry to wet monsoon years.
3. Exceedance warnings carry substantial economic value under a cost–loss analysis once their
   probabilities are recalibrated on a rolling window, which, to our knowledge, is the first such
   assessment for groundwater forecasts.

The work fits the journal's interest in hydrological prediction, uncertainty and decision support. All
code, results and figures are reproducible from public data with one script, and every number in the
manuscript is generated from the archived results.
